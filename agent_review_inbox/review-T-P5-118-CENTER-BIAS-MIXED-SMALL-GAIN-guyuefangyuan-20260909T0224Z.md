# T-P5-118 — nonzero center bias: mixed small-gain instead of false homogeneous closure

## 0. Bottleneck

T-P5-116 gives a zero-floor homogeneous small-gain theorem when the same Lyapunov
storage controls the anchor displacement by

`m Q_Z(delta) <= V`.

T-P5-117 proves that a genuine center offset

`delta = x + b`

cannot satisfy that homogeneous comparator at the zero-storage state unless the
bias is null in `Q_Z`.  It therefore leaves a mathematically sharp fork:

- bias-null: use T-P5-116;
- non-null bias: an additive/mixed channel is unavoidable.

This child closes the second fork.  It preserves as much of the homogeneous
second-jet structure as possible and pays only the genuinely bias-generated
floor.  The resulting checker gates use additions, multiplications, natural
squares and order only; no square root, inverse metric, eigenvalue or division is
needed by the trusted consumer.

No source identity, deployed center key, physical coverage, Lean receipt,
verifier result, admission or registry promotion is claimed.

---

## 1. Weighted quadratic bias split

Let `Q` be any PSD quadratic form.  For vectors `x,b` and positive real tuning
weights `r,s`, the exact identity

`(r+s)(s Q(x) + r Q(b)) - r s Q(x+b) = Q(s x - r b)`

implies

**(1.1)**

`r s Q(x+b) <= (r+s)(s Q(x) + r Q(b))`.

This is the root-free two-block Young inequality in a form suited to exact
rational certificate tuning.  The weights are not physical parameters; a search
layer may choose small positive rationals while the checker verifies (1.1)
algebraically.

Now assume the centered displacement and bias obey

**(1.2)** `m Q(x) <= V`, with `m>0`,

**(1.3)** `Q(b) <= B`, with `B>=0`.

Then (1.1) gives

**(1.4)**

`r s m Q(delta) <= (r+s)(s V + r m B)`, where `delta=x+b`.

All terms are nonnegative, so squaring is sound:

**(1.5)**

`r^2 s^2 m^2 Q(delta)^2`
`<= (r+s)^2 (s V + r m B)^2`.

On a candidate storage sublevel `0<=V<=R`,

`V^2 <= R V`,

hence

**(1.6)**

`r^2 s^2 m^2 Q(delta)^2`
`<= (r+s)^2 [ A V + C ]`,

with the exact rational coefficients

**(1.7)** `A = s^2 R + 2 r s m B`,

**(1.8)** `C = r^2 m^2 B^2`.

The decomposition is structurally useful: the center bias creates an `O(B)`
relative-rate surcharge through `A`, but the irreducible additive term is
`O(B^2)`.  Since `B=Q(b)` is already quadratic in the bias vector, the additive
floor generated by this second-jet channel is quartic in the center-offset norm.

## 2. Consume the homogeneous second-jet power packet without pretending the bias vanishes

Use the T-P5-116 pointwise power packet, with all quantities on the same physical
anchor segment / storage domain:

**(2.1)**

`4 gamma d p_A^2 <= h Q(delta)^2 Qd`,

where `gamma>0`, `d>0`, `h>=0`, `Qd>=0`.

Multiplying by `r^2 s^2 m^2` and applying (1.6) yields

**(2.2) MIXED BIASED-ANCHOR POWER PACKET**

`4 gamma d r^2 s^2 m^2 p_A^2`
`<= h (r+s)^2 (A V + C) Qd`.

This is the key bridge.  It is strictly different from collapsing the entire
anchor error to a cell-wide absolute constant: the centered part still scales
with `V`, while only the true center mismatch generates the constant `C`.

## 3. Division-free mixed absorption theorem

Suppose the nominal Lyapunov ledger is

**(3.1)** `Vdot <= -c V - Qd + p_A`,

with `c>=0`.

Choose nonnegative desired charges `alpha,beta,theta`.  Check the two exact
coefficient gates

**(3.2) relative gate**

`h (r+s)^2 A <= 16 gamma d r^2 s^2 m^2 alpha theta`,

**(3.3) additive gate**

`h (r+s)^2 C <= 16 gamma d r^2 s^2 m^2 beta theta`.

Combining (2.2)-(3.3) gives

`p_A^2 <= 4 theta (alpha V + beta) Qd`.

The square identity

**(3.4)**

`(alpha V + beta + theta Qd)^2`
`- 4 theta (alpha V + beta) Qd`
`= (alpha V + beta - theta Qd)^2 >= 0`

therefore yields

**(3.5)** `p_A <= alpha V + beta + theta Qd`.

Substitution into (3.1) proves

**(3.6) MIXED SMALL-GAIN CONCLUSION**

`Vdot <= -(c-alpha) V - (1-theta) Qd + beta`.

Thus if

`alpha < c`, `theta < 1`,

both strict storage decay and strict dissipation reserve survive, but a genuine
nonzero center bias is honestly represented by the additive floor `beta`.

The trusted checker need not divide by `m`, `gamma`, `d`, `r`, `s`, or `theta`.
It checks (3.2)-(3.3) exactly as cross-multiplied rational inequalities.

### Simplification of the pure bias gate

Because `C=r^2 m^2 B^2`, positive `r,m` allow the mathematical simplification

**(3.7)**

`h (r+s)^2 B^2 <= 16 gamma d s^2 beta theta`.

For a typed implementation, keeping the uncancelled gate (3.3) is often safer
because it avoids a separate nonzero-cancellation lemma; (3.7) is useful for
budget design.

## 4. Simple equal-weight corollary

Taking `r=s=1` gives the easy-to-implement fallback

`m Q(delta) <= 2(V+mB)`

and on `V<=R`

`m^2 Q(delta)^2 <= 4[(R+2mB)V + m^2 B^2]`.

Hence sufficient gates are

**(4.1)** `h (R+2mB) <= 4 gamma d m^2 alpha theta`,

**(4.2)** `h B^2 <= 4 gamma d beta theta`,

which again imply (3.6).

This equal-weight form is intentionally a fallback.  If `B=0`, it loses a
factor because it still applies a generic two-vector split to a one-vector
problem.  When the source proves the bias is exactly null, T-P5-116 should be
used directly; the biased theorem must not replace the sharper homogeneous
lane.

For `B>0`, rational `r/s` is a certificate-quality knob.  Large `r/s` makes the
coefficient on the centered part approach the homogeneous coefficient while
charging the bias more heavily; smaller ratios do the opposite.  This allows a
producer to trade relative rate loss against additive floor without introducing
square roots into the trusted layer.

## 5. Invariant / ultimate-tube interface

Let

`lambda = c-alpha > 0`.

Dropping the nonpositive `-(1-theta)Qd` term from (3.6) gives

`Vdot <= -lambda V + beta`.

Therefore a proposed rational storage radius `R0` can be checked without
performing the division `beta/lambda`: simply require

**(5.1)** `beta <= lambda R0`.

At `V=R0`, one has `Vdot<=0` (indeed `<=-(1-theta)Qd`).  With the usual
same-domain/outer-collar first-exit premise, this makes `V<=R0` a forward
invariant ultimate tube.

This is the correct semantic outcome of a non-null center bias.  The bias-null
branch can have zero floor; the biased branch has a rationally certified tube
unless additional signed cancellation removes the bias contribution.

## 6. Hard obstruction: zero-floor contraction does not follow when the bias packet is genuinely nonzero

The additive floor is not merely an artifact of the weighted inequality.
Consider the scalar exact data

`gamma=d=h=1`, `V=0`, `Qd=1`, `Q(delta)=B=4`, `p_A=2`.

Then the T-P5-116-style pointwise packet is exactly saturated:

`4 p_A^2 = 16 = Q(delta)^2 Qd`.

The nominal ledger permits

`Vdot <= -Qd + p_A = 1`.

Thus the premises themselves allow an outward derivative at the zero-storage
state.  In particular, no theorem of the form

`Vdot <= -c_keep V - d_keep Qd`

with `d_keep>0` can follow from these data alone.  Equivalently, a claim
`p_A<=theta Qd` with `theta<1` is false here because it would require `2<1`.

So for a genuine nonzero bias one needs at least one of:

1. an additive budget such as `beta` above;
2. a source-proved signed cancellation that constrains the sign/correlation of
   `p_A` beyond its square bound;
3. a redesigned anchor/storage center that makes the bias null.

Retuning the homogeneous T-P5-116 parameters cannot remove this obstruction.

## 7. Signed-correlation refinement

The weighted split should itself be regarded as a fallback if the source can
retain the signed cross term.  Since

`Q(x+b)=Q(x)+Q(b)+2 B_Q(x,b)`,

a one-sided upper bound on the signed cross term can be inserted before any
Young split.  Negative correlation may reduce or even cancel the bias charge.

Therefore the preferred source/CSE order is:

1. form the actual signed `Q(delta)` or its relevant Gram expression;
2. preserve a one-sided cross enclosure if available;
3. only if correlation is unavailable, consume the rational `(r,s)` fallback.

This is consistent with T-P5-114's general rule that intervalization should
happen after forming the signed energy object whenever possible.
