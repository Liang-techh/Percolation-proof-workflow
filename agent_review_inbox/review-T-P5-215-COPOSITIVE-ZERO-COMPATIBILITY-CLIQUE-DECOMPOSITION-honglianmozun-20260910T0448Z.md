# T-P5-215 — copositive zero compatibility / clique decomposition

## 0. Verdict and seam closed

**CONDITIONAL_PASS_MATHEMATICAL_CHILD / pending source binding.**

T-P5-214 proves that the nonnegative zero set of an indefinite copositive Schur block is a finite union of supportwise PSD-kernel cones, and it correctly warns that zero rays from different supports cannot be freely mixed. Its first recommended optimization child is a sound graph/clique or maximal-support pruning rule.

This child gives that rule exactly.

For a symmetric copositive matrix `K`, any two nonnegative zero vectors `x,y` satisfy

`x^T K y >= 0`.

Moreover their whole conic span remains in the zero set **iff**

`x^T K y = 0`.

Using T-P5-214 complementarity, this is equivalently a support test:

`x^T K y=0  <=>  supp(y) subseteq {j : (Kx)_j=0}`.

Thus the positive inactive residual of a zero ray is an exact incompatibility mask, not merely a local KKT diagnostic.

Given any finite complete zero-ray generator packet, build a graph with one vertex per ray and put an edge exactly when the cross energy vanishes. Then:

1. every clique spans a genuine cone of zeros;
2. every zero vector is generated by a clique;
3. it is enough to retain maximal cliques to cover the complete zero set;
4. incompatible rays can be rejected by the residual mask before any larger support/PSD merge is attempted.

Finally, on the flat-active T-P5-213/T-P5-214 second-order branch, a positive debit witness never needs three or more zero-ray atoms. Under the inherited common-zero copositivity hypotheses, existence of any positive-debit endpoint zero direction is equivalent to existence of either

- one zero ray with positive self debit, or
- two **compatible** zero rays whose pair combination has positive debit.

So once a complete atomic zero-ray packet exists, the second-order witness search is only a one-ray / compatible-pair scan. Maximal-clique enumeration is useful for the exact zero-set representation, but is not needed to decide existence of a T-P5-210 second-order witness.

## 2. T215-A — cross energy of two copositive zeros is nonnegative

### Theorem A

Let `x,y in Z_+(K)`. Then

**`x^T K y>=0`.**

### Proof

For every `t>=0`, `x+t y>=0`, hence copositivity gives

`0 <= (x+t y)^T K (x+t y)`.

Because both endpoints are zeros,

`x^T K x=0`, `y^T K y=0`,

so

`0 <= 2t x^T K y`.

For any `t>0`, divide by `2t` and obtain

`x^T K y>=0`.

QED.

This is the key sign fact that prevents cancellation between different zero rays. All cross-energy terms among zero atoms are one-sided nonnegative.

## 3. T215-B — exact compatibility criterion for conically mixing two zero rays

### Theorem B

Let `x,y in Z_+(K)`. The following are equivalent:

1. `x^T K y=0`;
2. `x+y in Z_+(K)`;
3. every nonnegative combination `a x+b y`, `a,b>=0`, lies in `Z_+(K)`.

### Proof

For `a,b>=0`,

`(a x+b y)^T K(a x+b y)`

`=a^2 x^T Kx + 2ab x^T Ky+b^2 y^TKy`

`=2ab x^T K y`.

By Theorem A the remaining scalar is nonnegative.

- If `x^TKy=0`, every nonnegative combination has zero energy.
- If `x+y` has zero energy, then `2x^TKy=0`.
- Statement 3 trivially implies statement 2.

QED.

Define two zero rays to be **compatible** when `x^TKy=0`.

Compatibility is symmetric because `K` is symmetric. It is reflexive on zero rays, but it is generally not transitive; therefore the correct global object is a graph, not an equivalence relation.

## 4. T215-C — inactive residual is an exact support incompatibility mask

Let `x in Z_+(K)` and define its nonnegative residual

`r(x):=Kx>=0`.

Define

`F(x):={j : r(x)_j=0}`,

`P(x):={j : r(x)_j>0}`.

T-P5-214 complementarity gives

`supp(x) subseteq F(x)`.

### Theorem C

For every `y in Z_+(K)`,

**`x` and `y` are compatible iff `supp(y) subseteq F(x)`.**

Equivalently,

**`supp(y) cap P(x)=emptyset`.**

### Proof

By symmetry,

`x^T K y=(Kx)^T y=r(x)^T y`.

Every summand `r(x)_j y_j` is nonnegative. Therefore their sum is zero iff each coordinate with `r(x)_j>0` has `y_j=0`.

QED.

Thus a positive omitted-row residual is stronger than a local first-order barrier: it globally forbids every compatible zero ray from activating that coordinate.

### Corollary C1 — PSD-support superset pruning

Suppose `S=supp(x)` and `U superseteq S`. If `K_UU>=0` and the zero-extended `x_U` belongs to `ker(K_UU)`, then

**`U subseteq F(x)`.**

Indeed a PSD zero must be a kernel vector, so `(Kx)_j=0` for every `j in U`.

Therefore no candidate larger PSD zero cone containing `x` needs to be tested if its support intersects `P(x)`.

This is an exact support-pruning rule available before any expensive kernel-generator expansion on that candidate superset.

## 5. T215-D — finite zero-ray compatibility graph

Assume a finite family of nonzero zero rays

`v_1,...,v_N in Z_+(K)`

is **complete in the following sense**:

for every `z in Z_+(K)` there exist coefficients `a_i>=0` such that

`z=sum_i a_i v_i`.

T-P5-214 supplies such a packet by taking the union of complete finite generators of all surviving supportwise PSD kernel cones. Redundant rays are allowed.

Construct the graph `G_K` on vertices `{1,...,N}` with an edge `{i,j}` exactly when

`v_i^T K v_j=0`.

Equivalently, by Theorem C,

`supp(v_j) subseteq F(v_i)`.

### Theorem D1 — every clique spans a zero cone

If `C` is a clique and `a_i>=0` for `i in C`, then

**`sum_{i in C} a_i v_i in Z_+(K)`.**

### Proof

Expand the quadratic energy. Self terms vanish because every `v_i` is a zero. Every pairwise cross term vanishes because `C` is a clique. Therefore the total energy is zero.

QED.

### Theorem D2 — every zero uses only mutually compatible active generators

Let

`z=sum_i a_i v_i in Z_+(K)`, `a_i>=0`.

Then the active index set

`I(z):={i:a_i>0}`

is a clique.

### Proof

Expanding,

`0=z^TKz=2 sum_{i<j} a_i a_j v_i^T K v_j`.

By Theorem A, every cross energy `v_i^TKv_j` is nonnegative. Every coefficient product on the active set is strictly positive. A sum of nonnegative terms can vanish only if every active pair has zero cross energy.

QED.

### Theorem D3 — exact clique-cone cover

Under completeness of the generator packet,

**`Z_+(K)=union_{C clique in G_K} cone{v_i:i in C}`.**

Because every finite clique lies in some maximal clique,

**`Z_+(K)=union_{C maximal clique in G_K} cone{v_i:i in C}`.**

The maximal-clique cover need not be disjoint or unique when the generator packet is redundant. No uniqueness claim is needed for sound dispatch.

## 6. Relation to T-P5-214 supportwise PSD cones

T-P5-214 represents the zero set as a union over supports `S` with `K_SS>=0` of

`C_S=iota_S(ker(K_SS) cap R_+^S)`.

Inside one such cone, any two vectors have zero cross energy. Indeed, if `x,y in C_S`, then

`K_SS x_S=K_SS y_S=0`,

hence

`x^T K y=x_S^T K_SS y_S=0`.

So all generators belonging to one supportwise PSD-kernel cone form a clique automatically.

The new content concerns **different** support packets. They may or may not be compatible. Theorem C gives the exact residual-mask test, and Theorem D shows that compatible packets may be merged safely while incompatible packets must remain separated.

A cone-level merge test is therefore:

Let `V_A,V_B` be generator matrices for two already-certified zero cones. Then

**`cone(V_A union V_B) subseteq Z_+(K)` iff `V_A^T K V_B=0` entrywise.**

Under copositivity, there is no hidden cancellation that could make a nonzero cross block harmless.

## 7. T215-E — maximal cliques are sufficient, but second-order witness search needs only pairs

Now return to the flat-active T-P5-213/T-P5-214 second-order branch.

Assume the inherited completed-square and common-zero hypotheses, including:

- endpoint zero tangents are generated linearly from reduced zero vectors;
- the active-kernel debit branch `E=N^TPN` is zero, so no pure-active positive-debit witness remains;
- each compatible reduced zero cone has a debit pullback that is copositive by the T-P5-210 small-perturbation argument.

For every zero-ray atom `v_i`, let `L_i` denote its corresponding lifted endpoint zero tangent after the fixed Schur/active-kernel choice. Define the debit Gram entries

`d_ij:=L_i^T Q L_j`.

For a compatibility clique `C`, the pullback matrix

`D_C:=(d_ij)_{i,j in C}`

is copositive on `R_+^C`.

T-P5-214 would test whether `D_C` is nonzero. The next theorem makes the witness itself sparse.

### Theorem E — one-ray / compatible-pair witness theorem

Assume the complete zero-ray packet above. There exists an endpoint zero tangent `w` with

`w^T Q w>0`

iff at least one of the following holds:

**(A) One-ray witness:** there exists `i` with

`d_ii>0`.

**(B) Compatible-pair witness:** there exist distinct compatible `i,j` such that

`d_ii=d_jj=0`

and

`d_ij>0`.

In case (B), the explicit witness is `L_i+L_j`, with

`(L_i+L_j)^TQ(L_i+L_j)=2d_ij>0`.

### Proof

The reverse direction is immediate.

For the forward direction, let `w` be a positive-debit endpoint zero tangent. By Theorem D2 its reduced component can be expressed using generators from one compatibility clique `C`. Therefore the corresponding coefficient vector `a>=0` satisfies

`a^T D_C a>0`.

Because `D_C` is copositive, each diagonal entry satisfies `d_ii>=0`.

- If some `d_ii>0`, case (A) holds.
- Otherwise every diagonal is zero. For any distinct `i,j in C`, copositivity of the two-coordinate restriction gives

  `0 <= (e_i+t e_j)^T D_C(e_i+t e_j)=2t d_ij`

  for every `t>0`, so `d_ij>=0`.

  If every off-diagonal entry were also zero, then `D_C=0`, contradicting `a^TD_Ca>0`. Hence some `d_ij>0`, giving case (B).

QED.

### Consequence

After a complete atomic zero-ray packet has been constructed, **existence** of a T-P5-210 second-order endpoint witness can be decided by:

1. scan each atom for positive self debit;
2. scan compatible atom pairs for positive cross debit.

No triple, higher-dimensional clique optimization, eigenvector search, or generic copositivity optimizer is logically needed for witness existence.

Maximal cliques remain useful when one needs the complete zero-set geometry or a whole reduced cone, but they are unnecessary for the yes/no second-order witness decision.

## 8. Exact rational implementation packet

For rational `K`, rational zero-ray generators, and rational lifted debit data, the whole child is exact rational.

For each atom `v_i`:

1. verify `v_i>=0`, `v_i^TKv_i=0`;
2. compute the exact residual `r_i=Kv_i`;
3. record the zero mask `F_i={j:(r_i)_j=0}` and positive mask `P_i={j:(r_i)_j>0}`.

For each pair `(i,j)`:

4. reject compatibility immediately if `supp(v_j)` intersects `P_i`;
5. otherwise compatibility is exact and `v_i^TKv_j=0` follows;
6. if second-order debit is being searched, compute `d_ii`, `d_jj`, `d_ij` exactly.

A FAIL witness against an attempted merge is simply a pair with

`v_i^TKv_j>0`.

Then

`(v_i+v_j)^TK(v_i+v_j)=2v_i^TKv_j>0`,

so the merge is provably not a zero cone.

A positive second-order witness is either the exact rational tangent `L_i` or `L_i+L_j` from Theorem E.

No square roots, eigenvectors, pseudoinverses, nonlinear roots, or floating-point tolerances are required.

## 9. Regression A — T-P5-214 nonconic example becomes a graph with no edge

Take

`K=[[0,1],[1,0]]`.

For `x,y>=0`,

`[x,y]^T K[x,y]=2xy>=0`,

so `K` is copositive and indefinite.

The two zero atoms are

`v_1=e1`, `v_2=e2`.

Their cross energy is

`v_1^TKv_2=1>0`.

Thus the compatibility graph has two isolated vertices and no edge. The maximal-clique cover is exactly

`cone(e1) union cone(e2)`,

which is the true zero set.

The residual mask is also exact:

`Ke1=(0,1)`,

so `P(e1)={2}` and every zero compatible with `e1` must avoid coordinate 2.

## 10. Regression B — an indefinite copositive matrix with two overlapping maximal zero cones

Take

`K=[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,0]]`.

Then for `x,y,z>=0`,

`[x,y,z]^TK[x,y,z]=2xy>=0`.

The matrix is indefinite with eigenvalues `1,-1,0`.

The atomic zero rays are

`e1,e2,e3`.

Cross energies are

`e1^TKe2=1`,

`e1^TKe3=0`,

`e2^TKe3=0`.

Therefore the compatibility graph is the path

`e1 -- e3 -- e2`,

with maximal cliques `{e1,e3}` and `{e2,e3}`.

The exact zero set is

**`cone(e1,e3) union cone(e2,e3)`.**

This regression shows all of the intended geometry at once:

- the global zero set is not one cone;
- different support cones may merge along compatible directions;
- maximal cliques give the exact finite cover;
- the common ray `e3` may belong to more than one maximal zero cone.

## 11. Regression C — positive second-order debit can require a compatible pair even when every atom has zero self debit

Keep the endpoint zero geometry of Regression B and focus on the compatible clique `{e1,e3}`.

Take a reduced debit form

`G=[[0,0,1],[0,0,0],[1,0,0]]`.

For nonnegative coordinates,

`z^T G z=2z1 z3>=0`,

so `G` is copositive.

On the two zero atoms,

`e1^TGe1=0`,

`e3^TGe3=0`,

but

`e1^TGe3=1>0`.

Thus neither atom individually gives positive debit, while their compatible sum does:

`(e1+e3)^TG(e1+e3)=2>0`.

This is why a one-ray-only search is incomplete. Theorem E proves that adding compatible **pairs** is sufficient; triples are never necessary for existence under the inherited copositive pullback semantics.

## 12. Regression D — why compatibility must be checked in endpoint energy, not debit energy

Using Regression A's endpoint matrix

`K=[[0,1],[1,0]]`,

suppose an unrelated debit matrix happens to have a favorable cross term between `e1` and `e2`.

That does not permit their combination, because endpoint compatibility fails first:

`(e1+e2)^TK(e1+e2)=2>0`.

The correct order is therefore:

**endpoint zero compatibility (`K`) -> only then debit test (`Q/G`).**

A positive debit cross term between incompatible endpoint rays is irrelevant to T-P5-210 because their sum is not an endpoint zero tangent.

## 15. Boundaries deliberately left open

### Boundary A — complete atom generation

This child reduces the geometry after a complete finite zero-ray packet exists. It does not by itself eliminate the T-P5-214 work required to certify all supportwise PSD-kernel generators. The residual mask can prune candidate supersets, but completeness still must be proved.

### Boundary B — graph size

The pairwise compatibility graph has `O(N^2)` exact tests. Maximal-clique enumeration can still be exponential in the worst case. Theorem E avoids maximal-clique enumeration for the yes/no second-order witness question, but not for tasks requiring the full zero-set decomposition.

### Boundary C — copositivity is essential

Without copositivity, cross energies between zero vectors may have either sign and different cross terms may cancel. Then the clique theorem fails. Do not use this packet on an arbitrary indefinite quadratic with isolated zeros.

### Boundary D — debit pullback copositivity is inherited, not automatic from endpoint compatibility

The one-ray/pair theorem uses the T-P5-210 common-zero small-perturbation semantics to know that each compatible-clique debit pullback is copositive. Endpoint compatibility alone does not prove debit copositivity.
