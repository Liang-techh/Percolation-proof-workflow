# T-P5-241 — One-dimensional fiber quartic closure

## 0. Verdict

**CONDITIONAL_PASS_MATHEMATICAL_CHILD / pending source binding.**

T-P5-239/T-P5-240 reduce an affine-quadratic debit over a rank-one two-cap source intersection to an outer scalar coordinate and a transverse trust-region problem. When the transverse dimension is one — in particular for quotient dimension two — the remaining trust-region multiplier and secular equation are unnecessary.

This child gives an exact closed form. The complete fiber test uses only:

- one quadratic `W` deciding whether the concave vertex lies inside the fiber;
- one quadratic `V` in the interior-vertex branch;
- one quadratic `E` and one quartic `P` in the boundary branch.

No square root is retained in the certificate. Consequently the universal outer-coordinate check is a finite one-variable sign problem of degree at most four on each active source-radius branch.

For rational 2D source data with irrational generalized eigenvalues, the same packet lives in the exact quadratic field generated by one generalized eigenvalue. Sturm/root-isolation can then be performed over that ordered quadratic field; floating eigenvectors are not mathematically required.

# Part I — exact scalar-fiber maximum

## 1. Scalar fiber

Let `m>0`, `R>=0`, and consider the exact fiber

`F_R := { y in R : m y^2 <= R }`.

Let the target debit on this fiber be

`q(y) := A + 2 L y + G y^2`,

where `A,L,G` are real scalars. Define

`W := G^2 R - m L^2`,

`V := (-G) A + L^2`,

`E := m A + G R`,

`P := E^2 - 4 m L^2 R`.

The four quantities have different jobs:

- `W` is the vertex-feasibility gate;
- `V` is the exact vertex value after clearing the positive denominator `-G`;
- `E` is the signed boundary baseline after clearing the metric denominator;
- `P` is the square-root-free boundary discriminant.

## 2. Theorem A — exact vertex-versus-boundary classifier

For `m>0` and `R>=0`,

`q(y) <= 0` for every `y` with `m y^2<=R`

if and only if one of the following exact branches holds.

### Branch A: concave vertex lies in the fiber

If

`G<0`

and

`W = G^2 R - m L^2 >= 0`,

then the stationary point

`y_* = -L/G`

satisfies `m y_*^2<=R`, and

`max_{F_R} q = A - L^2/G`.

Hence safety is equivalent to the single denominator-free inequality

**`V = (-G)A + L^2 <= 0`.**

### Branch B: maximum lies on the boundary

If Branch A does not apply, the maximum is attained at a fiber endpoint. Therefore

`max_{F_R} q`

`= A + G R/m + 2 |L| sqrt(R/m)`.

Multiplying by the positive scalar `m`, this is nonpositive exactly when

`E + 2 |L| sqrt(mR) <= 0`.

The square root can be eliminated without loss, but only if the sign gate is retained:

**`E <= 0`**

and

**`P = E^2 - 4 m L^2 R >= 0`.**

Thus Branch B safety is exactly the pair `(E<=0, P>=0)`.

### Proof

If `G<0`, `q` is concave and its unconstrained maximizer is `-L/G`. The condition that this point belongs to the fiber is

`m L^2/G^2 <= R`,

which is exactly `W>=0`. Substitution gives `A-L^2/G`; multiplying by `-G>0` gives the equivalent sign condition `V<=0`.

If the concave vertex is outside the interval, `q` is monotone on the interval and its maximum is at one endpoint. If `G>=0`, `q` is convex (or affine), so its maximum on the compact interval is also at an endpoint. The larger endpoint value chooses the sign of `y` agreeing with `L`, yielding the displayed boundary expression.

For the final equivalence, let `S:=2|L|sqrt(mR)>=0`. Then `E+S<=0` iff `E<=0` and `(-E)^2>=S^2`, i.e. `E<=0` and `E^2>=4mL^2R`.

QED.

## 3. Tie and degenerate cases are already covered

- If `R=0`, the fiber is `{0}` and the theorem reduces to `A<=0`.
- If `G=0`, Branch A is impossible and Branch B is exact for the affine target.
- If `L=0`, the boundary packet reduces correctly to the sign of `A+GR/m` whenever the convex/boundary branch applies.
- If `G<0` and `W=0`, the vertex lies exactly on an endpoint. The Branch-A and Branch-B packets are equivalent there, so a checker may deterministically assign the tie to either branch.

This removes the singular/hard-case trust-region bookkeeping that T-P5-239 needs in higher transverse dimension: in one transverse dimension the direct scalar formula is complete.

# Part II — outer-coordinate polynomial closure

## 4. Structure inherited from T-P5-239/T-P5-240

On one fixed source-radius branch, the rank-one two-cap decomposition has an outer coordinate `s` and a one-dimensional transverse coordinate `y` satisfying

`m y^2 <= R(s)`,

with constant `m>0` and a radius-square polynomial `R(s)` of degree at most two. In the concrete rank-one source packet, `R(s)` is one of two affine functions of `s^2`.

The affine-quadratic target has exactly the form

`q_s(y)=A(s)+2L(s)y+G y^2`,

where

- `A(s)` has degree at most two;
- `L(s)` has degree at most one;
- `G` is constant.

For a fixed active source branch define

`W(s) := G^2 R(s) - m L(s)^2`,

`V(s) := (-G) A(s) + L(s)^2`,

`E(s) := m A(s) + G R(s)`,

`P(s) := E(s)^2 - 4 m L(s)^2 R(s)`.

Then

`deg W <= 2`,

`deg V <= 2`,

`deg E <= 2`,

and critically

**`deg P <= 4`.**

No higher-degree secular polynomial is needed.

## 5. Theorem B — exact universal safety reduction

Let `I` be any outer-coordinate interval on which one source-radius polynomial `R(s)` is active and nonnegative.

Then

`q_s(y)<=0` for every `s in I` and every `y` with `m y^2<=R(s)`

is equivalent to the following pointwise polynomial conditions:

- wherever `G<0` and `W(s)>=0`, require `V(s)<=0`;
- everywhere else, require `E(s)<=0` and `P(s)>=0`.

Thus the full 2D fiber safety problem is an exact semialgebraic sign problem involving only quadratics and quartics.

## 6. Finite root-partition certificate

For the intersection of two rank-one source caps, first partition the feasible outer interval by the roots of the source switch polynomial

`S(s):=R_1(s)-R_2(s)`

and by source feasibility endpoints. On each resulting source branch, partition again by the roots of `W(s)` when `G<0`.

Every open cell then has fixed:

1. active source radius `R_j`;
2. vertex-versus-boundary regime.

The checker only needs polynomial sign certificates:

- vertex cell: `V<=0`;
- boundary cell: `E<=0` and `P>=0`.

Because the relevant degrees are at most four, exact Sturm/subresultant or exact root-isolation machinery is enough. This is not a relaxation: failure of one of these polynomial signs produces an actual outer coordinate and therefore an actual positive-debit fiber witness after root isolation/back-substitution.

# Part III — the quotient-dimension-two algebraic branch

## 7. Why this closes the mathematical part of T-P5-240's 2D warning

T-P5-240 correctly observes that for rational `2x2` positive-definite source matrices `B1,B2`, a scalar `a` satisfying

`rank(B2-aB1)=1`

may be quadratic irrational. In dimension two this is generic rather than exceptional: `a` is simply one generalized eigenvalue.

Let

`p(t):=det(B2-t B1) in Q[t]`.

If `B1,B2` are positive definite and nonproportional, then after conceptual whitening the symmetric `2x2` generalized operator has two distinct positive eigenvalues. Hence `p` has two distinct positive real roots `a,mu`; they generate a field of degree at most two over `Q`.

Choose one root `a` and work in the ordered exact field

`F := Q(a)`.

Then all T-P5-240 quantities

`D=B2-aB1`, pivot data `(d,c)`, the solve `B1 v=c`, `kappa`, `mu`, a one-column transverse basis `Z`, the scalar metric `m=Z^T B1 Z`, and the target coefficients `A(s),L(s),G`

belong to `F` exactly.

Theorem B therefore produces `W,V,E,P in F[s]` of degrees at most `2,2,2,4` respectively.

No floating generalized eigenvector or matrix square root is mathematically necessary.

## 8. Exact sign/root checking over the quadratic field

Represent `F` by a rational minimal polynomial for `a` plus an isolating interval selecting the intended real root. Every field element is represented exactly by a reduced linear polynomial `u+v a`.

The sign of a field element is decidable exactly from the isolating interval; arithmetic and polynomial Euclidean division remain exact in `F`.

Therefore a Sturm sequence for `W,V,E,P` may be constructed directly over the ordered field `F`. Evaluations at rational interval endpoints return elements of `F`, whose signs are exact. Rational bisection isolates every relevant real root because `Q` is dense in `R`.

Consequently the 2D algebraic branch can use the same finite root-partition contract as the rational branch. The coefficient field changes; the inequality architecture does not.

This is substantially smaller than introducing a generic floating eigensystem plus a separate interval proof around every derived matrix coefficient.

## 9. Rational dominance/crossing gate before entering the algebraic field

The source geometry can still be classified over `Q` before constructing `a`.

Let

`Delta1 := B2-B1`.

Then

- first cap is contained in second iff `Delta1<=0`;
- second cap is contained in first iff `Delta1>=0`;
- in the genuine 2D crossing branch, `Delta1` is indefinite.

For a symmetric `2x2` matrix, indefiniteness is equivalent to

**`det(B2-B1)<0`.**

This is an exact rational crossing gate. Only after crossing is confirmed does the checker need one algebraic generalized eigenvalue to build the axial/transverse decomposition.

# Part IV — exact counterexamples that fix the checker contract

## 10. Endpoint-only checking in the transverse variable is unsound

Take

`m=1`, `R=1`, `A=1/2`, `L=0`, `G=-1`.

Then

`q(y)=1/2-y^2` on `[-1,1]`.

Both endpoints give `q(±1)=-1/2<=0`, but the interior vertex gives

`q(0)=1/2>0`.

Here `W=1>=0` and `V=1/2>0`, so the vertex gate catches the failure exactly.

Therefore no checker may replace Theorem A by endpoint sampling when `G<0`.

## 11. Squaring the boundary inequality without the sign gate gives a false PASS

Take

`m=1`, `R=1`, `A=3`, `L=1`, `G=0`.

Then

`E=3`,

`P=E^2-4L^2R=5>=0`,

but

`max q = 3+2=5>0`.

Thus `P>=0` alone is not a certificate. The unsquared sign gate **`E<=0` is mandatory**.

This is the exact analogue of the sign-loss bug that appears whenever a square-root inequality is cleared by squaring.

## 12. Checking only outer source endpoints is also unsound

Take the outer interval `s in [-1,1]` with

`m=1`,

`R(s)=1-s^2`,

`A(s)=-1`,

`L(s)=3/5`,

`G=0`.

At the outer endpoints `s=±1`, the transverse fiber collapses to `y=0`, so the target value is `-1` and both endpoints pass.

At `s=0`, however, `|y|<=1` and

`max q = -1 + 6/5 = 1/5>0`.

The boundary quartic packet detects this because

`E(s)=-1`,

`P(s)=1-(36/25)(1-s^2)`

`= (36 s^2-11)/25`,

which is negative near `s=0`.

Therefore the outer closure must certify polynomial sign on the whole interval; checking only source-switch points and interval endpoints is insufficient.

## 13. Exact irrational 2D regression

Take

`B1=I`,

`B2=[[2,1],[1,1]]`.

Both matrices are rational and positive definite. The generalized characteristic polynomial is

`p(t)=t^2-3t+1`,

with irrational roots

`a=(3-sqrt(5))/2`,

`mu=(3+sqrt(5))/2`.

Moreover

`det(B2-B1)=det([[1,1],[1,0]])=-1<0`,

so the two unit caps are genuinely crossing by the rational gate above.

For `a=(3-sqrt(5))/2`,

`D=B2-aI`

has rank one. Choosing the first diagonal pivot gives

`d=(1+sqrt(5))/2`,

`c=(d,1)^T`,

and the exact identity

`dD=cc^T`

because the lower-right entry reduces using `sqrt(5)^2=5`.

Thus this pair is a concrete source geometry that the rational-only `a` search in dimension two would miss, while the ordered quadratic-field + quartic-fiber packet handles it exactly.
