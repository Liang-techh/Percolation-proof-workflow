# T-P5-257 — Radial quotient-factor certificate

## 0. Verdict

**CONDITIONAL_PASS_MATHEMATICAL_CHILD / pending source binding.**

T-P5-256 closed a structured route to the **mixed** Hessian Gram packet

`B(s,theta)^T B(s,theta) <= kappa q(theta) G`,

but explicitly left open the weaker radial fallback

`||B(s,theta) theta||^2 <= kappa q(theta) (theta^T G theta)`.

The mixed packet has a strong kernel consequence: every `v in ker(G)` must be
annihilated by `B(s,theta)` for every ambient `theta`.  The radial packet does
**not** require that.  It only requires the contracted vector

`F(s,theta) := B(s,theta) theta`

to vanish when the **base point** `theta` itself lies in the quotient kernel.

This child isolates that distinction and gives an exact rational route:

1. compress `D` to an independent row basis;
2. use radial safety to prove that the polynomial `F` belongs to the linear
   ideal generated by the quotient coordinates;
3. factor **before** taking a Gram majorant,

   `F(s,theta) = A(s,theta) u`, `u = Dbar theta`;

4. compare the resulting quotient Gram only with the positive definite row-space
   metric `W`, not with the ambient singular `G`;
5. optionally normalize any ellipsoid/SOS certificate so every square factor
   itself carries one quotient coordinate.

The factor-before-Gram route is strictly stronger than the mixed route.  A
2D Hessian example below has a sharp radial constant `kappa=4` and an exact
single-square certificate, while the mixed matrix packet fails for every finite
`kappa` because of a kernel test vector.

No actual deployed Hessian/source packet, cell/tube/trajectory coverage,
Float64/interval semantics, Lean receipt, independent verification, admission,
registry mutation, or parent closure is claimed.

# Part I — rational quotient coordinates

## 1. Row-space compression

Let

`D in Q^(p x m)`, `rank(D)=r>0`.

Choose `Dbar in Q^(r x m)` whose rows are a basis of the row space of `D`.
Then there is a rational matrix

`L in Q^(p x r)`

with full column rank such that

**(1.1)** `D = L Dbar`.

Define

`u := Dbar theta`,

`W := L^T L`.

Since `L` has full column rank,

**(1.2)** `W > 0`,

and exactly

**(1.3)**

`theta^T D^T D theta = ||D theta||^2 = u^T W u`.

Thus the singular ambient Gram `G=D^T D` is replaced, without a square root or
pseudoinverse, by the positive definite quotient metric `W`.

Everything is rational.  `Dbar` and `L` may be obtained by exact row reduction.

## 2. Basis covariance

If another rational row basis is chosen,

`Dbar' = U Dbar`, `U in GL_r(Q)`,

then

`u' = U u`,

`L' = L U^(-1)`,

`W' = U^(-T) W U^(-1)`.

Hence any quotient quadratic packet transforms by congruence.  In particular,

`u^T H(theta) u`

is represented in the primed basis by

`u'^T [U^(-T) H(theta) U^(-1)] u'`.

So the construction is quotient-covariant; it does not depend on an arbitrary
orthonormalization.

# Part II — radial safety forces an exact polynomial factor

## 3. Setup

Let

`q(theta)=theta^T M theta`, `M=M^T>=0`,

and fix `R>0`.  Let

`F(s,theta) in Q[s,theta]^k`

be a polynomial vector; in the T-P5-255/256 application,

`F(s,theta)=B(s,theta) theta = E H_b(s theta)[theta,theta]`.

Assume there exists `kappa>=0` such that for every

`s in [0,1]`, `q(theta)<=R`,

**(3.1)**

`||F(s,theta)||^2 <= kappa q(theta) ||D theta||^2`.

Because `M>=0`, the right side is nonnegative.

## 4. Theorem 1 — radial kernel necessity

Under (3.1),

**(4.1)** `F(s,eta)=0`

for every `s in [0,1]` and every `eta in ker(D)` satisfying `q(eta)<=R`.

This is immediate because `D eta=0` makes the right side zero.

Since `R>0`, the set

`ker(D) intersect {q<R}`

contains a relative neighborhood of the origin in `ker(D)`.  Therefore, for
each component of the polynomial `F`, restriction to `ker(D)` vanishes on a
nonempty relative-open set.  As `s` also ranges over the open interval `(0,1)`,
polynomial identity gives

**(4.2)**

`F(s,eta)=0 for all real s and all eta in ker(D)`.

Thus a claimed radial packet on a centered positive-radius ellipsoid has a
**necessary exact algebraic gate**: the contracted polynomial must vanish on
the whole quotient kernel.

## 5. Theorem 2 — exact polynomial row-space factorization

With the same rational `Dbar`, condition (4.2) is equivalent to the existence of
a polynomial matrix

`A(s,theta) in Q[s,theta]^(k x r)`

such that

**(5.1)**

`F(s,theta) = A(s,theta) Dbar theta = A(s,theta) u`.

### Constructive proof

Choose a rational right inverse `R0` of `Dbar` and a rational kernel basis `N`
so that

`Dbar R0=I_r`, `Dbar N=0`,

and the square matrix

`T=[R0 N]`

is invertible.  Write

`theta = R0 u + N z`.

After this rational change of variables, each component of `F` becomes a
polynomial

`Ftilde(s,u,z)`.

The kernel identity is exactly

`Ftilde(s,0,z)=0`.

Hence every monomial of every component contains at least one quotient variable
`u_i`.  Assign each monomial to one such `u_i`, factor that `u_i` out, and collect
columns.  This constructs a polynomial matrix `Atilde(s,u,z)` satisfying

`Ftilde=Atilde u`.

Pulling back by `T^(-1)` gives (5.1).  No Groebner basis, pseudoinverse, numerical
rank decision, or square root is required.

The converse is immediate because `u=0` on `ker(D)`.

## 6. Exact fail classifier

The same construction yields a useful checker route.

After substituting `theta=R0 u+Nz`, compute the exact polynomial remainder

`Rem(s,z) := Ftilde(s,0,z)`.

- If `Rem=0`, the polynomial factorization exists and radial closure proceeds to
  the quotient metric.
- If `Rem!=0`, then the claimed radial inequality (3.1) is mathematically false
  on every centered positive-radius source ellipsoid on which it is supposed to
  hold for all `s in [0,1]`.

Indeed a nonzero rational polynomial cannot vanish on the open product
`(0,1) x` a relative neighborhood of `0` in the kernel.  Rational points are
dense, so one can choose a rational `s` and an arbitrarily small rational kernel
point with `Rem!=0`; there the right side of (3.1) is exactly zero while the left
side is positive.

This is an actual counterexample route, not merely a certificate-search failure.

# Part III — factor before Gram

## 7. The quotient radial packet

Once

`F=A(s,theta)u`,

the desired inequality becomes exactly

**(7.1)**

`u^T A(s,theta)^T A(s,theta) u
 <= kappa q(theta) u^T W u`.

Equivalently,

**(7.2)**

`u^T [kappa q(theta) W - A^T A] u >=0`.

The crucial point is that (7.2) is a scalar inequality only on the **realized
quotient vector** `u=Dbar theta`.  It does not ask the ambient Hessian map
`B(s,theta)` to annihilate all test vectors in `ker(D)`.

A sufficient quotient-matrix gate is

**(7.3)**

`A^T A <= kappa q(theta) W`,

but (7.2) is the exact radial obligation and may hold even when (7.3) is too
strong.

## 8. Theorem 3 — coefficient outer-Gram after quotient factorization

Suppose

`A(c)=sum_i c_i A_i`

for fixed matrices `A_i : Q^r -> Q^k`.  Let `S` be the stacked map

`S v=(A_1 v,...,A_t v)`.

Assume a source coefficient packet

**(8.1)** `c c^T <= q(theta) C`, `C>=0`.

Define

`H_C := S^T (C tensor I_k) S`.

By the completely-positive transport used in T-P5-256,

**(8.2)** `A(c)^T A(c) <= q(theta) H_C`.

Therefore the single rational PSD comparison

**(8.3)** `H_C <= kappa W`

implies the radial packet

**(8.4)**

`||F||^2 <= kappa q(theta) ||D theta||^2`.

This is the **factor-before-Gram principle**:

`contracted Hessian -> quotient-ideal factor -> coefficient Gram -> quotient metric`.

It can succeed after the original mixed Hessian Gram route has failed, because
kernel-incompatible columns of `B` may cancel after contraction with the same
`theta`.

# Part IV — fraction-free affine/linear coefficient corollary

## 9. Theorem 4 — adjugate linear-factor certificate

Assume now `M=M^T>0` is rational and

`q(theta)=theta^T M theta`.

Let

`d=det(M)>0`, `J=adj(M)`.

Suppose the quotient factor is linear in `theta`:

**(9.1)**

`A(theta)=sum_j theta_j A_j`.

Let `S_A` stack the `A_j` and define

**(9.2)**

`H_A := S_A^T (J tensor I_k) S_A`.

The exact ellipsoid rank-one inequality from T-P5-256 is

`d theta theta^T <= q(theta) J`.

Consequently,

**(9.3)**

`d A(theta)^T A(theta) <= q(theta) H_A`.

Hence the entirely rational PSD gate

**(9.4)**

`H_A <= kappa d W`

implies

**(9.5)**

`||F(theta)||^2 <= kappa q(theta) ||D theta||^2`.

No inverse of `M` or `W` is serialized.  The checker needs only determinant,
adjugate, exact matrix products, and PSD tests.

For higher-degree polynomial `A`, the graded tensor/outer-Gram construction of
T-P5-256 can be applied **after** the quotient factorization.  This preserves
radial cancellations that are invisible to the mixed matrix packet.

# Part V — quotient-normalized SOS certificate

## 10. Why an ordinary scalar SOS should retain the quotient factor

Define

`p(s,theta) := kappa q(theta) ||D theta||^2 - ||F(s,theta)||^2`.

After Theorem 2, `p=0` on `ker(D)`.

Suppose an SOS engine produces the standard source certificate

**(10.1)**

`p = sigma0 + (R-q) sigmaR + s(1-s) sigmaS`,

where each `sigma*` is a sum of polynomial squares.

On the open set

`s in (0,1)`, `theta in ker(D)`, `q(theta)<R`,

we have `p=0`, while every term on the right of (10.1) is nonnegative and the
two scalar multipliers are strictly positive.  Therefore every individual
square factor occurring in `sigma0`, `sigmaR`, and `sigmaS` vanishes on that
open kernel slice.  By the same linear-ideal argument as Theorem 2, each square
factor is divisible by the quotient coordinate vector `u`.

Thus every certificate of the form (10.1) can be normalized to

**(10.2)**

`p = u^T [Q0 + (R-q) QR + s(1-s) QS] u`,

where `Q0,QR,QS` are SOS matrix polynomials.

This gives a quotient-covariant SOS/Gram certificate rather than an ambient one.
Under `Dbar'=U Dbar`, the matrix packet transforms by

`Q' = U^(-T) Q U^(-1)`.

### Important boundary

This is a **normalization theorem for an existing SOS certificate**.  It is not a
claim that every nonnegative radial polynomial has a certificate of the simple
form (10.1).  Therefore failure of a bounded-degree SOS search after the exact
kernel factorization is `CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, not a mathematical FAIL.

The only exact FAIL gate supplied here is a nonzero polynomial kernel remainder
from Section 6.

# Part VI — strict separation from the mixed Hessian packet

## 11. Sharp 2D Hessian counterexample

Take

`theta=(x,y)`,

`q=x^2+y^2`,

`D=[1,0]`, so `G=diag(1,0)`, `u=x`, `W=1`.

Let the scalar nonlinear map be

`b(x,y)=x y`.

Then `b(0)=0`, `J_b(0)=0`, and its constant symmetric Hessian is

`H=[[0,1],[1,0]]`.

For the radial Hessian contraction,

`B(theta)=[y,x]`,

`F(theta)=B(theta)theta=2xy`.

The exact quotient factorization is

**(11.1)** `F=(2y)u`.

Since `y^2<=q`,

**(11.2)**

`F^2 = 4x^2y^2 <= 4 q x^2 = 4 q ||D theta||^2`.

Thus `kappa=4` proves the radial packet.  It is sharp: for `x!=0`,

`F^2/[q ||D theta||^2] = 4 y^2/(x^2+y^2)`,

whose supremum is `4` as `x/y -> 0`.

The residual polynomial is actually a single quotient square:

**(11.3)**

`4 q x^2 - F^2 = 4 x^4 = (2 x u)^2`.

### Mixed packet failure

The mixed T-P5-255 packet with any finite constant would require

`B(theta)^T B(theta) <= kappa q G`.

But test it on the kernel vector `v=e_2`.  Then

`v^T G v=0`,

while

`||B(theta)v||^2=x^2`.

For every point with `x!=0`, the mixed inequality fails regardless of `kappa`.
For `kappa=4`, the matrix difference is

`[[4x^2+3y^2, -xy],[-xy,-x^2]]`,

whose lower-right entry is negative and whose determinant is

`-4 x^2(x^2+y^2)`.

Therefore:

**radial Hessian safety can be exact and sharp even when the mixed Hessian Gram
packet is impossible.**

This is the concrete reason the T-P5-256 fallback is mathematically distinct.

## 12. Endpoint consequence in the same example

On `q<=R`,

`b(theta)^2=x^2 y^2 <= R x^2 = R ||D theta||^2`.

This matches the T-P5-255 endpoint constant `delta_b(R)=kappa R/4=R` for
`kappa=4`.  So the weaker radial Hessian route closes exactly the endpoint
leakage needed by T-P5-253 in this example, despite total failure of the mixed
Jacobian/Hessian kernel condition.

# Part VII — a second necessary boundary

## 13. Kernel divisibility alone does not buy the `q` factor

Take again

`D=[1,0]`, `q=x^2+y^2`, `u=x`,

but let

`F(x,y)=x=u`.

Then the exact quotient factorization exists, yet no finite `kappa` can satisfy

`F^2 <= kappa q u^2`

on any neighborhood of the origin: along `y=0`, `x!=0`, it becomes

`1 <= kappa x^2`.

Thus two logically different gates are required:

1. **kernel gate:** `F in <Dbar theta>`;
2. **order/Gram gate:** the quotient coefficient `A` must itself carry enough
   source-smallness, e.g. an outer-Gram packet proportional to `q`.

In the actual Hessian application, `E J_b(0)=0` is what makes the contracted
second-order term compatible with this scaling; it must not be silently dropped.
